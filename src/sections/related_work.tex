\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related-work}
\subsection{Traffic Representation and Cross-Flow Semantics}
\Budget{0.55 page}

Traffic representation learning differs in the information retained for detection.
NetMamba pretrains a Mamba encoder on packet bytes~\cite{R06}.
Yu et al. encode protocol-defined fields, layers, packets, and sessions, using hierarchical order recovery to learn structural dependencies~\cite{E01}.
Protocol-semantic methods further express the meaning of protocol fields.
Ku et al. translate BACnet services, objects, and values into operational statements for rule- and knowledge-assisted LLM detection~\cite{R12}.
MISSION extends IPFIX with MQTT attributes, correlates communications into event logs, and detects deviations from mined process models~\cite{R13}.
These semantic pipelines define operation interpretation and correlation mechanisms for their respective protocols.

Cross-flow modeling expands the evidence available for classification.
Tracegram treats a trace as a collection of flows and combines flow encoders with temporal aggregation for trace classification and key-flow attribution~\cite{R01}.
Huang et al. construct cross-flow contexts and inject endpoint, port, protocol, and statistical relations into attention for target-flow classification~\cite{R07}.
These approaches encode temporal or relational dependencies through learned representations.
Our representation makes operations, resources, and response outcomes explicit across protocols, then organizes related interactions and their order into bounded behavioral evidence for a target flow.

\subsection{Semantic Decisions and Computational Allocation}
\Budget{0.45 page}

Semantic detectors differ in how they convert input evidence into decisions.
From Flows to Words converts flow features into text, adds deterministic behavioral flags, and uses grammar-constrained generation to return a JSON verdict and attack score, with a threshold calibrated on development data~\cite{R14}.
Jev-IDS serializes one flow per request and obtains an attack probability and a finite-choice traffic category through Jev's typed interface~\cite{R02}.
Grammar-constrained generation controls the output format, while typed inference directly returns the requested probabilities.
Building on single-flow typed detection, our framework supplies Jev with operational semantics and related-flow evidence.

Computational allocation also depends on when semantic processing is invoked and what role its output serves.
MeeDet uses Mamba-based early exits to reduce average detection computation and applies an asynchronous LLM module to explain generated alerts~\cite{E03}.
Its LLM supports post-alert analysis, while the Mamba backbone determines detection outcomes.
In our framework, semantic decisions participate in final target-flow classification through two parallel paths: alert verification reassesses fast-detector alerts to improve precision, and cross-flow discovery independently selects candidates from the full stream to improve recall.
Together, bounded evidence construction and path-specific selection control the context processed and the scope of semantic inference.
